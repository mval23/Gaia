# GAIA — MONET-INSPIRED TASK MANAGER & DAILY PLANNER

Design a high-fidelity, desktop-first task manager and daily planning web application called **Gaia**.

Gaia combines:

* The calm atmosphere and colors of Claude Monet’s *Water Lilies*
* The precision and simplicity of Apple interface design
* Modern Liquid Glass surfaces
* A highly practical task manager
* A drag-and-drop 24-hour time-blocking calendar

Gaia should feel like a quiet digital environment for organizing time: beautiful and personal enough to enjoy opening every day, but structured and efficient enough to function as a serious productivity tool.

The overall feeling should be:

**Calm · Dreamy · Organized · Premium · Soft · Modern · Focused · Personal**

---

# 1. CRITICAL INFORMATION HIERARCHY

The organizational hierarchy must be understood exactly as follows:

**GROUP → CATEGORY → TASK**

There are three top-level groups:

1. **Work**
2. **Personal**
3. **Other**

These are the highest organizational level in Gaia.

## GROUP EXAMPLES

### Work

Contains work-related categories such as:

* Client A
* Client B
* Client C
* Internal
* Other clients/projects

### Personal

Contains personal categories such as:

* University
* Health
* Home
* Projects
* Finance

### Other

Contains categories that do not naturally belong under Work or Personal.

---

## CATEGORY EXAMPLES

Categories exist **inside groups**.

For example:

WORK
→ Client A
→ Client B
→ Client C

PERSONAL
→ University
→ Health
→ Personal Projects

OTHER
→ Miscellaneous

Categories should generally represent a **client, project, area, or meaningful responsibility**.

---

## TASKS

Tasks exist **inside categories**.

Example:

WORK
→ Client A
→ Prepare presentation
→ Review monthly report
→ Send documentation

→ Client B
→ Update Power BI dashboard
→ Client follow-up

PERSONAL
→ University
→ Algorithms assignment
→ Study statistics

The interface must never confuse groups and categories.

**Work, Personal, and Other are groups.**

**Client A, Client B, Client C, University, Health, etc. are categories.**

**Individual actions are tasks.**

---

# 2. VISUAL DIRECTION

Use Monet’s *Water Lilies / Nymphéas* series as inspiration for the visual identity.

Suggested palette:

* Misty powder blue
* Pale sky blue
* Soft sage
* Muted eucalyptus green
* Water-lily pink
* Dusty lavender
* Warm ivory
* Pearl white
* Very subtle blue-grey

The interface itself should be strongly inspired by Apple:

* Generous whitespace
* Large rounded corners
* Precise spacing
* Minimal visual clutter
* SF Pro / system-style typography
* Thin, elegant icons
* Soft depth
* Smooth transitions
* Clear information hierarchy

Use Liquid Glass selectively for:

* Navigation
* Floating controls
* Popovers
* Selected controls
* Side sheets
* Small overlays
* Calendar blocks where appropriate

Liquid Glass surfaces can include:

* Translucency
* Background blur
* Subtle refraction
* Soft highlights
* Thin semi-transparent borders
* Restrained shadows
* Layered depth

Do not turn every card into glass.

Content-heavy areas should remain exceptionally readable.

---

# 3. BACKGROUND — IMPORTANT

**The application's main background must be a solid or subtly tonal color, NOT a Monet painting or photographic image.**

Do not place a large painting behind the application.

Do not make the UI float over a full-screen Monet artwork.

Use a calm background color such as:

* Warm ivory
* Very pale blue-grey
* Soft mist blue
* Extremely subtle sage
* Pearl white

A very subtle gradient between related colors is acceptable, but the background should still visually read as a **color**, not an image.

This is important for keeping Gaia calm, clean, readable, and modern.

---

# 4. MONET ARTWORK AS SCATTERED DECORATIVE MOMENTS

Monet should still be visibly present in the product, but through **small, intentional decorative moments rather than the page background**.

Use small cropped fragments of public-domain Claude Monet paintings, especially the *Water Lilies / Nymphéas* series.

These decorative artwork fragments should be **scattered organically throughout the application**.

They may appear as:

* Small floating rectangular painting crops
* Soft rounded artwork tiles
* Narrow panoramic fragments
* Tiny visual accents beside page titles
* Decorative artwork near empty areas
* Small painting fragments around the edges of the workspace
* Occasional partially cropped artwork disappearing beyond a container edge

Do not arrange them in a rigid gallery.

They should feel almost like beautiful pieces of Monet have been casually placed throughout a personal planner.

Keep them away from dense functional areas so they never interfere with tasks, calendar events, controls, or text.

The artwork should remain clearly recognizable as Monet. Preserve visible:

* Brush strokes
* Water lilies
* Reflections
* Pond colors
* Willow trees
* Soft skies
* Blues
* Greens
* Lavenders
* Pinks

---

# 5. MONET DECORATIONS + PHRASES

Some scattered Monet artwork decorations should be paired with short, quiet phrases.

The phrases should feel reflective and calming rather than motivational or corporate.

Examples of the desired tone:

“one thing at a time”

“make room for what matters”

“a quiet day is still a full day”

“leave space”

“begin gently”

“there is time”

“slow is still moving”

“today, with intention”

“let the day unfold”

“focus on what is here”

These phrases may appear:

* Beside a small Monet crop
* Underneath an artwork fragment
* In an empty area between functional sections
* As subtle editorial text near the page title
* As occasional decorative text in the sidebar

Use them sparingly.

They should feel like handwritten notes or quiet editorial details, not productivity slogans.

A refined serif or subtle italic typeface may be used for these phrases, while all functional UI should remain in the system sans-serif.

---

# 6. ART DIRECTION BALANCE

The application must NOT look like:

* A museum website
* An art gallery
* A literal painting
* A scrapbook
* A generic productivity SaaS dashboard

The desired balance is approximately:

**75% Apple-inspired productivity application**
**15% Liquid Glass**
**10% Monet artwork and artistic details**

Monet provides emotion, atmosphere, color, and personality.

Apple-inspired design provides structure, clarity, and interaction.

Liquid Glass creates visual depth and connects the two.

---

# 7. PRIMARY APPLICATION STRUCTURE

Create three primary sections:

1. **Plan**
2. **Calendar**
3. **Manage**

Use a compact Apple-inspired navigation sidebar or navigation bar.

The navigation itself may use a subtle Liquid Glass treatment.

Also provide a quick way to switch or filter between:

**Work | Personal | Other | All**

This control does NOT represent categories.

It filters the application by the three top-level groups.

Use an Apple-style segmented control or compact selector.

---

# 8. PLAN VIEW

Plan is Gaia's primary workspace.

Its central purpose is:

**See what needs to be done → decide when to do it.**

Create a two-panel desktop layout:

**LEFT: Unscheduled tasks**

**RIGHT: 24-hour daily timeline**

The relationship between these two panels is the most important interaction in Gaia.

---

# 9. PLAN — LEFT TASK PANEL

Organize tasks according to the real Gaia hierarchy:

**Group → Category → Task**

Example:

WORK

▾ Client A · 3
○ Prepare presentation
○ Review monthly report
○ Send documentation

* Add task

▾ Client B · 2
○ Update dashboard
○ Client follow-up

* Add task

PERSONAL

▾ University · 2
○ Algorithms assignment
○ Study statistics

* Add task

Categories should appear as refined rounded sections or cards.

Each category includes:

* Expand/collapse chevron
* Category name
* Active task count
* Category color
* More-options menu
* Task list
* Inline “+ Add task”

The user should be able to collapse an entire group such as **Work** or **Personal**.

Individual categories can also be collapsed.

Completed tasks should become subtly desaturated and receive a refined strikethrough.

---

# 10. QUICK TASK CREATION

Task creation should be extremely fast.

Clicking:

**+ Add task**

inside Client A should create the task **inside the Client A category within Work automatically**.

An inline text input should immediately appear.

The user can type:

“Prepare Client A presentation”

and press Enter.

The task is created immediately.

Do not require a modal for basic task creation.

Advanced properties can be edited afterward.

---

# 11. TASK DETAILS

The expanded task editor can contain:

* Task name
* Group
* Category
* Due date
* Start time
* Duration
* Priority
* Notes
* Status

Use a compact Liquid Glass popover or side sheet instead of a large traditional modal.

Moving the task to another category should automatically update its group based on the destination category.

---

# 12. PLAN — 24-HOUR DAY PLANNER

The right side should contain a vertical 24-hour calendar.

Display:

00:00
01:00
02:00
...
23:00

Include:

* Current-time indicator
* Hour grid
* Half-hour subdivisions
* Existing calendar events
* Scheduled tasks
* Empty time slots

Visually emphasize normal waking hours while keeping all 24 hours accessible.

---

# 13. DRAG TASKS INTO THE DAY

Tasks from the left task panel can be dragged directly onto the calendar.

When dropped, they become scheduled time blocks.

The underlying task still belongs to:

**Group → Category → Task**

Scheduling it does not change its organizational hierarchy.

The calendar simply adds a time allocation to that task.

Time blocks should inherit their category color using soft, translucent backgrounds.

Users can:

* Drag a task onto the calendar
* Move it vertically to change its time
* Resize the top or bottom edge to change duration
* Move it between days
* Click it to edit
* Mark it complete
* Remove it from the schedule without deleting the task

During dragging or resizing, show:

**10:30 – 11:45**
**1h 15m**

Snap interactions to 15-minute increments.

The experience should feel close to manipulating events in Apple Calendar.

---

# 14. PLAN HEADER

At the top show:

**Today**
Sunday, September 13

Include:

* Previous day
* Next day
* Date picker
* Today shortcut when viewing another date

Show a lightweight summary such as:

**5 tasks · 4h 30m planned · 3h 15m free**

Keep this subtle.

Gaia is not an analytics dashboard.

A small Monet fragment and quiet phrase can appear near this header where there is enough whitespace.

---

# 15. CALENDAR VIEW

Create a dedicated calendar workspace with:

**Day | Week | Month**

using an Apple-style segmented control.

## DAY

Show the detailed 24-hour timeline.

## WEEK

Show seven vertical day columns with hourly rows.

Tasks and calendar events appear as elegant translucent blocks.

Allow users to:

* Drag between times
* Drag between days
* Resize duration
* Complete scheduled tasks

## MONTH

Use a clean month grid.

Show restrained colored task/event indicators.

Do not overcrowd cells.

Clicking a date should transition into its Day view.

---

# 16. MANAGE VIEW

Manage is the organizational workspace.

Provide three sections:

**Tasks | Categories | Groups**

---

# 17. TASK MANAGEMENT

Allow users to:

* Search tasks
* Filter by group
* Filter by category
* Filter by status
* Sort tasks
* Edit tasks
* Complete tasks
* Archive/delete tasks
* Move tasks between categories

Useful filters include:

**All Groups | Work | Personal | Other**

and then a category filter based on the selected group.

---

# 18. CATEGORY MANAGEMENT

Remember:

**Categories belong to Groups.**

Example:

Work
→ Client A
→ Client B
→ Client C

Personal
→ University
→ Health

Each category contains:

* Name
* Color
* Parent group
* Number of tasks
* Edit
* Reorder
* Archive/delete

Allow drag-and-drop reordering.

Allow categories to be moved between groups.

---

# 19. GROUP MANAGEMENT

Groups are the highest level of organization.

The default groups are:

**Work**
**Personal**
**Other**

Show each group with:

* Name
* Categories inside it
* Total active tasks
* Reorder controls where appropriate
* Collapse/expand

Do not introduce another organizational layer beneath categories.

The hierarchy remains:

**GROUP → CATEGORY → TASK**

---

# 20. INTERACTION QUALITY

Interactions should feel exceptionally polished.

Use:

* Smooth expand/collapse
* Spring-like drag interactions
* Gentle hover states
* Subtle scale changes
* Soft glass transitions
* Animated task completion
* Fluid calendar resizing
* Clear drop zones
* Smooth 15-minute snapping

Avoid excessive animation.

The experience should feel responsive, tactile, and physical in the spirit of Apple interfaces.

---

# 21. DESIGN DETAILS

## Cards

Use:

* Large rounded corners
* Ivory or pearl surfaces
* Very subtle borders
* Soft shadows
* Controlled translucency

## Typography

Use a clean system sans-serif similar to SF Pro for functional UI.

A refined serif may be used sparingly for:

* “Today”
* Page titles
* Decorative phrases accompanying Monet artwork

Never use decorative typography for task content.

## Icons

Icons should be:

* Thin
* Rounded
* Minimal
* Consistent in stroke weight
* Inspired by SF Symbols

---

# 22. CATEGORY COLORS

Category colors should come from the Monet palette.

For example:

**Client A — dusty lavender**
**Client B — powder blue**
**Client C — muted sage**
**University — water-lily pink**
**Health — pale eucalyptus**

These colors identify information without dominating the interface.

Group colors should not replace category colors.

Categories are the primary source of color-coding for tasks and calendar blocks.

---

# 23. CORE UX PRINCIPLE

Gaia should optimize for one central workflow:

**Capture → Organize → Plan → Execute → Complete**

The user should be able to:

1. Capture a task quickly.
2. Place it inside a category.
3. The category determines whether it belongs to Work, Personal, or Other.
4. Open Plan.
5. See unscheduled tasks beside the day's timeline.
6. Drag important tasks onto available time.
7. Resize them to determine how much time to dedicate.
8. Move them as plans change.
9. Complete them from either the task list or calendar.

The interface should minimize planning friction.

---

# 24. AVOID

Do not use:

* Full-screen Monet backgrounds
* Painting images as the main page background
* Incorrect Group → Category → Group → Task hierarchies
* Additional organizational layers between categories and tasks
* Dense productivity dashboards
* Excessive statistics
* Gamification
* Bright saturated colors
* Heavy borders
* Too many floating cards
* Excessive glass blur
* Generic SaaS aesthetics
* Kanban-first layouts
* Large traditional modals
* Artwork behind important text
* Decorative elements that interfere with drag-and-drop
* Museum/gallery aesthetics

---

# 25. FINAL DESIGN TARGET

The final product should feel like:

**“Apple Calendar and a beautifully designed personal task manager, reimagined with the quiet colors, softness, and artistic atmosphere of Monet’s Water Lilies.”**

The background is a calm **color**, not a painting.

Monet appears through **small scattered artwork fragments, painterly decorative details, the color palette, and occasional quiet phrases**.

The functional hierarchy is always:

**WORK / PERSONAL / OTHER**
↓
**CATEGORIES such as Client A, Client B, Client C, University**
↓
**TASKS**

Prioritize the **Plan view** above all others.

The most important visual and functional relationship to demonstrate is:

**Unscheduled tasks on the left → drag → scheduled time blocks on the 24-hour calendar on the right.**

Create a cohesive, high-fidelity interface with realistic tasks, category colors, calendar blocks, subtle Monet decorations, and polished Apple-inspired interactions.
