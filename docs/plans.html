Gaia Docs · Plans · a proposal, 18 September 2026

Two early sketches, one Gaia

Gaia's Compass

Before Gaia there were two plans. Compass was a life operating system that wanted to steer every hour. The Life Hub was a quieter plan: a two-minute morning check-in, a home for forgotten goals, and reviews that make you pause. Gaia is what both turned into once they learned to be kind. This proposal brings the best of each into Gaia and leaves the scoreboard behind.

Compass

Direction → priorities → projects → time blocks → habits → reviews. Six layers, fourteen screens, a local AI that proposes changes.

Brings: why things matter, flexible anchors, minimum versions, evidence.

Life Hub

Check-in, tasks, habits, goals, reviews, dashboard. Built to be opened every morning in two minutes, with an Ollama companion that only asks questions.

Brings: goal momentum, backfilling, a Socratic companion, phases gated by real use.

Gaia today

Plan, Calendar, Goals & habits, Manage. Tiny versions, rhythms instead of streaks, let go, a weekly reflection, four Monet palettes.

Keeps: the tone, and the rule that nothing counts what didn't happen.

The short answer

Don't build Compass beside Gaia. Let it become Gaia's sense of direction.

Gaia already does the middle of both plans: tasks, time blocks, habits with tiny versions and goal links, a weekly reflection. What it doesn't have yet is the top (why any of it matters), the moment of looking back, and a few quiet tools around the day itself. The two sketches fill different parts of that gap, and where they overlap they mostly agree. The proposal:

  1. Compass lives on as a name. The new Direction section (values, roles, one provisional sentence) is called Compass. Gaia is the garden, and the compass tells you which way to walk.
  2. Everything becomes three moves: know what matters → give it a place → look back kindly. Every idea from both plans fits under one of these.
  3. The Life Hub sets the pace. Its two-minute rule shapes the morning check-in, its momentum tags give goals an honest sense of progress, and its companion asks questions instead of handing you conclusions.
  4. Every new feature passes Gaia's three tests (below). Some ideas come in as they are, most are reshaped, and a few are left out, each with a reason.
  5. It ships in four small seasons, each one gated by real use, as the Life Hub roadmap suggested: the next season starts when the last one has become part of your week, not on a date.

The filter

Three questions every Compass idea has to answer

These come straight from Gaia's conventions: the copy rules in src/lib/copy.ts, the positive-only CheckIn type, and the reason closing the day was removed on 15 September.

Does it count what didn't happen?

Gaia stores only done, tiny and rest. There is deliberately no value for “missed”. A feature that needs one is reshaped until it doesn't.

Does it follow you from yesterday?

A day is chosen, not inherited. Nothing new may carry an unfinished pile into a fresh morning, or show one in the evening when it is least welcome.

Can you ignore it and lose nothing?

Values, check-ins, the companion: all optional. Gaia has to work fully for someone who never opens them, and never nag them to.

New · Today's light

Monet painted the same cathedral thirty times. Only the light changed.

Compass wanted energy, mood, sleep and stress on the dashboard as numbers. The Life Hub wanted a morning check-in for mood and sleep that takes under two minutes. Gaia joins them and takes one of its own paintings as the model: the Rouen Cathedral series is one building in many kinds of light, and none of them is the wrong light.

Today's light is a small card at the top of Plan in the morning: three taps, well inside the Life Hub's two minutes. It sits right where your Morning start habit already opens Gaia, so it rides an existing cue instead of needing a reminder. Every question is optional, and a day with no entry is simply not logged, just as with habits. An earlier day can be filled in later with no mark on it. It never produces a score.

Its one job is to offer a day shape. Low light offers a Gentle day (tiny versions, no figures, one essential). Good light offers a Bright day, which is the only time Gaia suggests adding a second focus block. You can always pick a different shape.

Over a month, Look back can show the light as a quiet band of colour next to what you tended. That's where patterns like “gym days tend to follow rested nights” can come up, as observations and never as rules.

New · From the Life Hub

Goals move. They don't get a percentage.

The Life Hub's plan started from a real problem: personal goals lived in a notebook and were never checked on, because nothing in the day led back to them. Gaia gives goals a home already. What it's missing is the check-in.

Once a week, right after the weekly reflection, Gaia asks how each active goal is moving. You pick one word. It's your own sense of it, not something calculated, so it stays honest for goals like “feel calmer at work” that can't be measured.

If you pick snagged, Gaia asks one more thing: on what? That's Compass's idea that an obstacle is usually about clarity, time, energy or setup, and that the answer is to adjust the setup, not to blame yourself. Each answer points to a small next step, such as a first task, a smaller tiny version, or a time block.

Goals that are genuinely countable can add an optional milestone (“12 of 20 chapters”). Everything you tag and write builds the goal's timeline, which is what the monthly look back reads from.

Left out, on purpose

What Gaia declines, and why

Streaks

A streak turns every blank day into a loss. Rhythms (“about 3 a week”) keep the goal and drop the punishment.

A 25-panel dashboard

It contradicts Compass's own principle: never overwhelm the user with excessive planning. Plan stays short and Look back opens on purpose.

Planned vs completed

Its only output is a gap. “Where the time went” shows the same blocks with no comparison.

“X of the last 30 days”

Kinder than a streak, but the denominator still counts blanks. Gaia counts only what you tended.

A daily review

Both plans had one. Gaia tried it as closing the day and removed it on 15 September, because it showed unfinished things at the hour they are least welcome.

A companion that summarises you

Compass's recommendations feed stays out. The Life Hub was right that a bot doing the review quietly replaces your own reflection.

Rebuilding on FastAPI and SQLite

The Life Hub planned a fresh stack. Gaia already runs, stores data locally and online, and works on a phone, so a rewrite would cost weeks and add nothing you'd see.

Social media & avoidance tracking

Counting minutes invites self-surveillance. “What pulled me away?” is an optional reflection question instead.

How it ships

Four seasons, each one useful on its own

Earlier seasons fit Gaia's current model with small, additive changes, and migrateState fills them in for old saves. Borrowing the Life Hub's best rule, a season starts when the last one has become part of your week, not on a date. Adding many new habits at once is a common reason people stop using a planner.

SeedsSeason 1 · built
  • Habit fields: why, if–then, coming back
  • The one that matters: one task marked with a star, plus up to two, on Plan
  • Capture with Ctrl I, a button or the sidebar, into an Inbox at the top of Later, sorted with one menu
  • With someone else: a folded section for tasks you're waiting on (“Laura has it · since Tue”)
  • Optional milestone count on goals
  • Install on the home screen (PWA manifest and icon)
  • The three habits above, offered in a Start with a few habits card in Settings, each one optional

Your call

Five decisions before Season 1

How private should Gaia be?

The two plans disagreed. Compass made local-first a core principle, and the Life Hub said privacy wasn't a concern. Gaia sits in between today: local by default, with optional accounts. The proposal keeps that, and asks only that mood and companion data stay on the device unless you choose otherwise.

“Snagged”, or the Life Hub's plainer “stuck”?

“Snagged” suggests something you can unhook, which fits the follow-up question. “Stuck” is more direct.

Is “Compass” the right name for the Direction section?

It honours where Gaia came from. The alternative is plainer: “What matters”.

Gentle · Steady · Bright, or keep Compass's three?

The proposal favours the light words because they match Today's light and make no day sound lesser.

Should check-ins sync in account mode?

Recommended: off by default, with one switch in Settings.

Which companion engine first?

Both plans chose Ollama, and you already run Gaia on your own computer, so Ollama first is the natural start. An in-browser model can come second, for when you open Gaia on your phone.

Small actions. Meaningful direction. Leave space.
